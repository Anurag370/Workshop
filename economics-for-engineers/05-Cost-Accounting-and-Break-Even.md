# 05 — Cost Accounting and Break-Even Analysis

> **Exam-critical chapter.** 2024-2025 alone contained **three** one-mark items (break-even point,
> contribution from P/V ratio, average fixed cost), **two** five-mark items (fixed vs variable cost;
> the two-year P/V ratio problem) and a **15-mark** four-part break-even question, plus the cost-sheet
> question.

## Learning objectives

After studying this chapter you should be able to:
1. Classify costs: fixed, variable, semi-variable; direct, indirect; recurring, non-recurring; sunk,
   opportunity, incremental.
2. Prepare a cost sheet: prime cost → work cost → cost of production → cost of sales → profit.
3. Explain **contribution** first, then P/V ratio, then break-even.
4. Compute break-even in units and in rupees, margin of safety, target-profit sales, and the selling
   price required for a target break-even point.
5. Solve the 2024-2025 Group-B Q5 and Q6 and the Group-C Q7 problems independently.

## 1. Prerequisites and basic concepts

- **Cost** = value of resources consumed. **Revenue** = value of output sold.
- **Profit** = revenue − cost. Everything else in this chapter is a smarter way of computing profit.

---

## 2. Explanation from zero

### 2.1 Cost behaviour

| Type | Definition | Behaviour | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** | Constant in total within the relevant range | Changes per unit inversely with volume | Rent, salaries, insurance, depreciation |
| **Variable** | Varies in direct proportion to activity | Constant per unit | Material per unit, power per unit, packaging |
| **Semi-variable** | Fixed element + variable element | Mixed | Electricity bill, telephone charges |

> **PYQ 2024-2025 B-Q4:** *Distinguish between variable cost and Fixed cost* [5] — see the model
> answer in Section 8.

### 2.2 Direct and indirect cost

- **Direct cost** can be traced without ambiguity to a product, job or unit (direct material, direct
  labour, direct expenses such as freight on a specific consignment).
- **Indirect cost** cannot be traced directly and is therefore apportioned (factory overhead,
  administration overhead, selling and distribution overhead, rent, insurance).

### 2.3 Recurring and non-recurring cost

- **Recurring cost** — incurred repeatedly each period as part of normal operation (materials, wages,
  rent, power).
- **Non-recurring (one-time / special) cost** — incurred once for a specific purpose (plant
  installation, a legal case, a special advertising campaign, relocation).

> **PYQ 2023-2024, B-Q4** asked exactly this pair; the 2024-2025 paper asked the fixed/variable pair.
> Learn both as a table.

### 2.4 Other important cost ideas

| Term | Meaning | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Sunk cost** | Already incurred, unrecoverable | Ignore |
| **Opportunity cost** | Value of the next best alternative forgone | Include |
| **Incremental / differential cost** | Difference in cost between alternatives | Use for comparison |
| **Avoidable cost** | Cost saved if the project is dropped | Include when deciding to drop |
| **Fixed cost (period cost) vs product cost** | Fixed costs are period costs; product costs are attached to units | Know in a cost sheet |

### 2.5 The cost sheet (sequence to remember)

```
Direct material
   + Direct labour
   + Direct expenses                = PRIME COST
   + Factory overhead               = WORK COST
   + Administration overhead        = COST OF PRODUCTION
   ± Opening stock / closing stock  = COST OF SALES
   + Selling & distribution overhead= COST OF SALES (fully loaded)
   then                             = GROSS PROFIT / PROFIT
```

| Term | Formula |
|---|---|
| Prime cost | Direct material + Direct labour + Direct expenses |
| Work cost | Prime cost + Factory overhead |
| Cost of production | Work cost + Administration overhead (+/− opening and closing stock adjustments as stated) |
| Cost of sales | Cost of production + selling and distribution overhead, adjusted for stock |
| Gross profit | Sales − Cost of goods sold |
| Net profit | Gross profit − other expenses (interest, depreciation adjustments, etc.) |

### 2.6 Contribution — the heart of break-even analysis

**Contribution** = what a sale contributes towards covering fixed costs and profit.

$$
\text{Contribution} = \text{Sales} - \text{Variable cost}, \qquad
\text{Contribution per unit} = SP - VC
$$

Think of it as "the first fixed cost it pays for, and then the profit it produces". Break-even analysis
then asks: *how much contribution do we need to cover the fixed costs?*

### 2.7 P/V ratio (contribution ratio)

$$
\text{P/V ratio} = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{Sales}} = 1 - \frac{\text{Variable cost}}{\text{Sales}}
$$

It is the **percentage of every sales rupee** that is available to cover fixed costs and profit.

> **PYQ 2024-2025, A-X:** *Determine contribution if sales is Rs 1,50,000 and P/V ratio is 40%.*
> Contribution $= 1,50,000 \times 0.40 = \mathbf{Rs\ 60,000}$.

### 2.8 Break-even point

The sales level at which total contribution exactly equals total fixed costs, so profit is zero.

$$
\text{BEP (units)} = \frac{FC}{SP - VC} = \frac{FC}{\text{Contribution per unit}}
\qquad
\text{BEP (sales, Rs)} = \frac{FC}{\text{P/V ratio}}
$$

### 2.9 Margin of safety (MOS)

How much sales can fall before the business starts losing money:

$$
MOS = \text{Actual (or budgeted) sales} - \text{BEP sales}
$$

Expressed as a percentage: $MOS\% = MOS/\text{actual sales} \times 100$. A high MOS means a safe
business.

### 2.10 Target profit and required selling price

$$
\text{Sales for target profit} = \frac{FC + \text{Target profit}}{\text{P/V ratio}}
$$

If the **selling price** itself must change to hit a target break-even point $Q_B$:

$$
\text{Required contribution/unit} = \frac{FC}{Q_B}
\qquad
\text{Required SP} = VC + \frac{FC}{Q_B}
$$

## 3. Formulas, variables and units

| Formula | Variables | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution $= S - VC$ | $S$ sales (Rs), $VC$ variable cost (Rs) | First step of every break-even question |
| Contribution/unit $= SP - VC$ | Rs/unit | BEP in units |
| P/V ratio $= (S-VC)/S$ | % | BEP in rupees |
| BEP units $= FC/(SP-VC)$ | units | Break-even quantity |
| BEP sales $= FC/(\text{P/V ratio})$ | Rs | Break-even revenue |
| MOS $= S - \text{BEP sales}$ | Rs (or %) | Safety margin |
| Target-profit sales $= (FC+P)/(\text{P/V ratio})$ | Rs | Planning |
| Required SP $= VC + FC/Q_B$ | Rs/unit | Price policy |
| Average fixed cost $= FC/Q$ | Rs/unit | 2024-2025 A-XII |
| Total cost $= FC + VC_{u}Q$ | Rs | Cost function |

## 4. Worked examples

### 4.1 Example 1 — contribution and BEP from a one-mark item (PYQ 2024-2025, A-III)

**Given:** selling price Rs 10/unit; variable cost Rs 6/unit; fixed cost Rs 5,000.
**Required:** break-even point.
**Formula:** contribution/unit $= SP - VC$; BEP units $= FC/(SP-VC)$.
**Substitution:** contribution $= 10 - 6 = 4$ per unit; BEP $= 5,000/4 = 1,250$ units.
BEP sales $= 1,250 \times 10 = 12,500$.
**Answer:** **1,250 units (Rs 12,500 of sales).**
**Reasonableness check:** at 1,250 units, contribution = Rs 5,000 which exactly equals the fixed cost;
below that the firm loses money.

### 4.2 Example 2 — P/V ratio and fixed cost from two years (PYQ 2024-2025, B-Q5)

**Given:** Year 2009 — Sales Rs 2,70,000, Profit Rs 6,000. Year 2010 — Sales Rs 3,00,000, Profit
Rs 15,000. Cost structure and selling prices unchanged.
**Required:** (a) P/V ratio; (b) fixed cost; (c) break-even point; (d) margin of safety at a profit of
Rs 24,000.
**Formula:** Contribution = FC + Profit, and Contribution = Sales × P/V ratio, so with two years
$\Delta P/\Delta S = $ P/V ratio; then $FC = P_1 - S_1 \times (\text{P/V ratio})$.
**Substitution (a):** P/V ratio $= (15,000 - 6,000)/(3,00,000 - 2,70,000) = 9,000/30,000 = 0.30$.
**(b):** $FC = 2,70,000 \times 0.30 - 6,000 = 81,000 - 6,000 = 75,000$.
*Check with year 2:* $3,00,000 \times 0.30 - 75,000 = 90,000 - 75,000 = 15,000$ ✓.
**(c):** BEP sales $= 75,000/0.30 = 2,50,000$.
**(d):** Two readings —
 - Margin of safety at the actual 2010 sales: $3,00,000 - 2,50,000 = 50,000$ (16.67% of sales).
 - Sales needed for a profit of Rs 24,000: $(75,000 + 24,000)/0.30 = 3,30,000$; MOS over the actual
   sales of Rs 3,00,000 = **Rs 30,000**.
**Answer:** P/V ratio **30%**; fixed cost **Rs 75,000**; BEP **Rs 2,50,000 of sales**; MOS **Rs 50,000**
at current sales, or **Rs 30,000** if the business must earn Rs 24,000 profit.
**Reasonableness check:** the profit increase of Rs 9,000 on extra sales of Rs 30,000 implies a 30%
contribution ratio — consistent; and with sales rising 11%, profit rose 150%, which is exactly what
operating leverage predicts near break-even.

### 4.3 Example 3 — cost sheet with profit (PYQ 2024-2025, B-Q6)

**Given (March 2005, Yuvraj & Co.):** raw materials consumed Rs 4,000; direct labour Rs 6,000; factory
overhead Rs 500; administration overhead 20% of work cost; selling and distribution overhead 6 paise per
unit; units produced 20,000; units sold 18,000 at a profit of 20% of selling price.
**Required:** cost sheet showing the profit.
**Formula/sequence:** work cost → administration overhead → cost of production → cost per unit → cost of
sales for the units sold → sales from the profit condition (Profit = 20% of sales ⇒ Sales = Cost of sales
/ 0.80).
**Substitution:**
- Work cost $= 4,000 + 6,000 + 500 = 10,500$.
- Administration overhead $= 0.20 \times 10,500 = 2,100$.
- Cost of production $= 10,500 + 2,100 = 12,600$; per unit $= 12,600/20,000 = Rs 0.63$.
- Cost of production of 18,000 units sold $= 18,000 \times 0.63 = 11,340$.
- Selling and distribution overhead on units sold $= 18,000 \times 0.06 = 1,080$.
- Cost of sales $= 11,340 + 1,080 = 12,420$.
- Profit = 20% of selling price ⇒ $S - 12,420 = 0.20S$ ⇒ $0.80S = 12,420$ ⇒ $S = 15,525$.
- Profit $= 15,525 - 12,420 = 3,105$; SP per unit $= 15,525/18,000 = Rs 0.8625$; profit per unit
  $= Rs 0.1725$.
**Answer:** sales **Rs 15,525**; cost of sales **Rs 12,420**; **profit Rs 3,105** for the month.
**Reasonableness check:** profit per unit Rs 0.1725 is exactly 20% of the selling price Rs 0.8625 ✓;
also 2,000 unsold units (Rs 1,260) remain as closing stock.
*(Assumption flagged: selling & distribution overhead is charged on units **sold**, the usual treatment.)*

### 4.4 Example 4 — the four-part break-even question (PYQ 2024-2025, C-Q7)

**Given:** sales 4,000 units at Rs 25 = Rs 1,00,000; materials consumed Rs 40,000; variable overheads
Rs 10,000; labour charges Rs 20,000; fixed overheads Rs 18,000; net profit Rs 12,000.
**Required:** (a) break-even units; (b) sales needed for a profit of 20% on sales; (c) extra units
needed to earn the present profit if the selling price is reduced by 20% and by 25%; (d) selling price
to bring break-even down to 500 units.
**Formula:** variable cost/unit = (materials + variable overheads + labour)/units = 70,000/4,000 =
Rs 17.50; contribution/unit = 25 − 17.50 = Rs 7.50; BEP = FC/contribution.
**Substitution (a):** BEP units $= 18,000/7.50 = 2,400$ units; BEP sales = 2,400 × 25 = Rs 60,000.
**(b):** Variable cost ratio $= 70,000/1,00,000 = 0.70$. Let sales $= S$:
$S - 0.70S - 18,000 = 0.20S \Rightarrow 0.10S = 18,000 \Rightarrow S = 1,80,000$ (7,200 units);
profit = Rs 36,000.
**(c):** Contribution required to preserve the present profit of Rs 12,000 = FC 18,000 + 12,000 =
Rs 30,000.
 - Price cut 20% ⇒ SP $= 20$; contribution/unit $= 20 - 17.50 = 2.50$; units $= 30,000/2.50 = 12,000$;
   **extra units = 12,000 − 4,000 = 8,000**.
 - Price cut 25% ⇒ SP $= 18.75$; contribution/unit $= 18.75 - 17.50 = 1.25$; units $= 30,000/1.25 =
   24,000$; **extra units = 24,000 − 4,000 = 20,000**.
**(d):** Contribution/unit needed for a 500-unit BEP $= 18,000/500 = 36$; required SP $= 17.50 + 36 =
$ **Rs 53.50 per unit**.
**Answer:** (a) **2,400 units**; (b) **Rs 1,80,000 (7,200 units)**; (c) **8,000 extra units at a 20%
cut and 20,000 extra units at a 25% cut**; (d) **Rs 53.50 per unit**.
**Reasonableness check:** every extra rupee of price cut destroys 5 units of demand per Rs 1 of
contribution lost; the numbers behave consistently.

> **Flagged alternative assumption:** if "labour charges Rs 20,000" is treated as **fixed**, then
> variable cost/unit = Rs 12.50, fixed cost = Rs 38,000, BEP = 3,040 units, sales for a 20% profit on
> sales = Rs 1,26,667 (5,066.67 units), extra units 2,666.67 and 4,000, and required SP for a 500-unit
> BEP = Rs 88.50. State the assumption you use.

### 4.5 Example 5 — average fixed cost and average total cost (PYQ 2024-2025, A-XII)

**Given:** 780,000 units produced; fixed costs Rs 32 lac (Rs 32,00,000); variable cost Rs 16 per unit.
**Required:** average fixed cost per unit (and average total cost).
**Formula:** $AFC = FC/Q$; $ATC = AFC + AVC$.
**Substitution:** $AFC = 32,00,000/780,000 = 41.0256$; $ATC = 41.03 + 16 = 57.03$.
**Answer:** average fixed cost **Rs 41.03 per unit**; average total cost **Rs 57.03 per unit**.
**Reasonableness check:** AFC must be far larger for a high-volume product, and must fall if volume
rises — e.g. at 780,000 units AFC is about 2.6× the variable cost per unit.

## 5. Common mistakes and how to avoid them

| Mistake | Why wrong | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Using sales instead of contribution in a BEP formula | Gives wrong break-even | BEP = FC ÷ contribution |
| Confusing P/V ratio with mark-up on cost | Mark-up on cost is $C$ based | P/V ratio is a % of **sales** |
| Forgetting to add back fixed cost in target-profit sales | Understates sales | Sales = (FC + profit)/P/V ratio |
| Computing extra units but forgetting to subtract current units | Overstates the extra volume | extra = required − present |
| Using the new price with the old contribution ratio in (c) | Wrong | Recompute contribution/unit at the new price |
| Adding selling overhead to the cost sheet per unit produced instead of per unit sold | Mismatches cost with revenue | Charge overhead on units sold |
| Dividing profit by sales when profit is stated "as % of selling price" | Wrong equation | Profit = 0.2 × Sales ⇒ Sales = Cost/0.8 |
| Treating depreciation as a variable cost | Distorts contribution | Depreciation is fixed (or excluded) unless the question says otherwise |

## 7. Five-mark theory answers in exam-ready format

### Q. Distinguish between variable cost and fixed cost. (PYQ 2024-2025, B-Q4)

| Basis | Fixed cost | Variable cost |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Constant in total within the relevant range | Varies in direct proportion to activity |
| Per-unit behaviour | Falls as volume rises (AFC = FC/Q) | Constant per unit |
| Total behaviour | Unchanged by volume | Changes with volume |
| Examples | Rent, salaries, insurance, depreciation, factory overhead | Material per unit, power per unit, packaging, piece-rate wages |
| Nature | Period cost | Product/part cost |
| Effect on profit | Affects profit in total only | Affects profit in total and per unit |
| Relevance range | Valid only inside a stated volume range | Usually valid over a wider range |
| Break-even role | The amount contribution must cover to avoid a loss | The reason contribution exists |

**Conclusion:** classification decides whether a cost behaves like a fixed or variable item in
break-even and profit analysis; a semi-variable cost must be split into its fixed and variable parts
before analysis.

### Q. Distinguish between recurring and non-recurring costs. (PYQ 2023-2024, B-Q4)

| Basis | Recurring cost | Non-recurring cost |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Occurs repeatedly every period | Occurs once for a specific purpose |
| Example | Materials, wages, rent, power, insurance | Plant installation, relocation, litigation, special campaign |
| In cost sheet | Part of normal operating cost | Treated as exceptional/special item |
| Planning | Included in routine budgets | Provided for separately as a contingency |
| Effect on profit | Reduces profit every period | Reduces profit in the year it is incurred |

**Conclusion:** for estimation and budgeting, recurring costs are forecast from activity levels while
non-recurring costs need a specific project justification.

### Q. "Cash flow is an example of an investment activity and is also considered as Sale of Investment by Non-Financial Enterprise" (T/F) — PYQ 2022-2023, A-VIII

**True.** Selling a long-term investment (shares, land, plant) by a non-financial enterprise is an
**investing activity** in the statement of cash flows, because it converts a non-current asset into
cash. (Compare: buying equipment is also investing; borrowing and repaying is financing; sales
receipts and payments to suppliers are operating.)

## 10. Practice questions with answers and explanations

**Q1.** A firm sells at Rs 100 per unit; variable cost Rs 60; fixed cost Rs 4,00,000 per year. Find the
BEP and the margin of safety if annual sales are 8,000 units. (Supplementary)
**A.** Contribution/unit = Rs 40; BEP units = 4,00,000/40 = **10,000 units** (Rs 10,00,000 sales).
MOS = 8,000 units × 100 − 10,00,000 = 8,00,000 − 10,00,000 = **−Rs 2,00,000**, i.e. the firm is
**operating at a loss** of Rs 80,000 (loss = (8,000−10,000) × 40).
*Check:* negative MOS correctly signals a loss.

**Q2.** Find the selling price at which a product with variable cost Rs 60 and fixed cost Rs 1,20,000
breaks even at 3,000 units. (Supplementary)
**A.** Required contribution/unit = 1,20,000/3,000 = Rs 40; SP = 60 + 40 = **Rs 100**.
*Check:* at Rs 100, contribution Rs 40 × 3,000 = Rs 1,20,000 = FC ✓.

**Q3.** Sales rise 10% from Rs 10,00,000 with P/V ratio 35% and fixed cost Rs 2,00,000. By how much does
profit change? (Supplementary)
**A.** Contribution at old sales = 3,50,000; profit = 1,50,000. New sales = 11,00,000; contribution =
3,85,000; profit = 1,85,000. **Increase = Rs 35,000** (equal to 10% × 3,50,000).
*Check:* operating leverage: contribution/sales = 35%, so a 10% sales rise lifts profit by 35% ✓.

**Q4.** Explain why a price cut requires disproportionately more units to hold profit constant.
**A.** A price cut of $x$ per unit reduces contribution/unit by $x$ but also lowers the contribution
earned on every unit already sold. To keep total contribution constant, the volume must rise by
$FC + \text{present profit}$ divided by the new, smaller contribution per unit — a nonlinear effect.
That is why even a small cut (e.g. 20%) can demand thousands of extra units.

## 11. Chapter summary

- Classify costs first; then compute contribution; then P/V ratio; then break-even.
- Cost sheet: prime → work cost → cost of production → cost of sales → profit.
- BEP units = FC ÷ (SP − VC); BEP sales = FC ÷ P/V ratio.
- Target-profit sales = (FC + profit) ÷ P/V ratio; required SP = VC + FC/Q_B.
- 2024-2025 answers: P/V 30%, FC Rs 75,000, BEP Rs 2,50,000, cost-sheet profit Rs 3,105,
  BEP 2,400 units, Rs 53.50 required SP.
